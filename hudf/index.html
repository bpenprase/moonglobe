About the image and the data

The image is the Hubble Ultra Deep Field released in 2004: about a million seconds of exposure with Hubble's Advanced Camera for Surveys, combining blue, green, red and near-infrared light. Credit: NASA, ESA, S. Beckwith (STScI) and the HUDF Team (ESA/Hubble heic0406a, CC BY 4.0).

Galaxy positions, brightness in 11 filters, and photometric redshifts come from the UVUDF catalog of Rafelski et al. (2015). Spectroscopic redshifts, stellar masses and star-formation rates come from the MUSE Hubble Ultra Deep Field survey (Bacon et al. 2023) and earlier surveys collected by Rafelski et al.

The press image has no coordinates of its own. They were found by matching about 3,800 galaxies in the image to the catalog, which ties each pixel to the sky to about 0.01 arcseconds. Each pixel is 0.030 arcseconds across. Distances and times use a flat universe with H0 = 67.7 km/s/Mpc and Ωm = 0.31 (Planck 2018).

A photometric redshift is estimated from a galaxy's colors and can be off by 5 to 10 percent, sometimes much more. A spectroscopic redshift is measured from spectral lines and is accurate to better than 0.1 percent.
