Ruler measurements

Choose Ruler, then click two points. To measure the major axis, click the two ends of the fitted orbit that are farthest apart.
    Each line is tagged with the star it snapped to. Use the menus to correct the star or say what you measured.
    The file lists every ruler line and every start/end motion, with star names and years. Open it in Excel or Google Sheets to work out the masses.

    Kepler's third law

    M = a³ / P², with a in AU and P in years, gives the mass in Suns.

    How to use this in class
    1. Press Play or drag the year slider. Start jumps back to 1995.5. The first two seconds show every star's name.
    2. Click on a star. Its name appears and the app traces where it has been, with year marks.
    3. Stop the movie at one year and press Mark start. Move to a later year and press Mark end. The panel reports how far the star moved in arcseconds and how many years it took.
    4. Trace a whole orbit (S0-2, S0-38 and S0-102 complete one or more), press Fit traced points, then use the Ruler to measure the major axis. Half of it is the semi-major axis a.
    5. Find the period P from the year marks, then use Kepler's third law to estimate the black hole's mass.
    6. Measure several stars, then press Save measurements (CSV) to keep the data and finish the calculations offline.

    North is up and east is to the left. Positions are offsets from Sgr A* (the white star symbol), using the movie's scale bar: 0.1″ = 120 movie pixels. Each orbit you see is tilted, so the ellipse on the sky is a projection of the true orbit.

    Movie: Keck/UCLA Galactic Center Group. Star positions were measured from every frame of the movie and smoothed with Kepler orbit fits. Built for Astronomy 150, Models of Time and Space.
