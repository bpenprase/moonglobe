These apps are part of a suite of programs developed for Bryan Penprase’s Astronomy 150 course at Soka University of America, entitled “Models of Time and Space.” Each one runs in a web browser on a computer, tablet or phone, with nothing to install.

The course

Models of Time and Space

This course introduces both astrophysics and physics, exploring models of time and space that describe the universe’s origins and evolution. The course will delve into both classical and modern concepts, starting with how people through the centuries found their place on earth, navigated across the largest expanses of space available to them in ships, and began to map the planet and the skies. These maps improved with time to include accurate scales and angular measurements that enabled precise navigation and precise positions of stars, planets, and galaxies. From this improved understanding arose the ideas of physics – the laws of Kepler and Newton and the stunning images produced by Galileo, Cassini and other astronomers. From these astronomical observations came an awareness of our location in a larger galaxy, and eventually the understanding of the vastness of the galaxy and the universe, and our origins in a cosmic Big Bang event that put the universe (and space and time) into motion.

The course also will explore some of the most interesting parts of physics that connect to astronomy, including Einstein’s theories of relativity, the Big Bang, black holes, particle physics, and the discovery of the Higgs Boson at CERN. Students will learn how these models help us understand the fundamental forces and particles that govern the universe, and key discoveries made possible by the development of particle accelerators, gravitational wave detectors, and new space telescopes. The course will balance theoretical understanding with practical exploration of astronomical and cosmological phenomena, empowering students to think critically about the universe and its underlying physics. The course will be taught at a level that minimizes the use of mathematics and explores the most profound concepts of how physics and astrophysics shape our conceptions of reality and have enabled us to map the physical universe from the smallest possible scales to the limits of the observable universe.

The course is based on the book Models of Time and Space from Astrophysics and World Cultures by Bryan Penprase.

The apps

Explore the sky, the Moon, Mars and the distant universe

Celestial Sphere

The whole sky on a sphere. Look out from the center as we see it from Earth, or turn it around like an antique celestial globe.

  • Stars, constellation figures and IAU boundaries
  • All 25 constellation plates of Flamsteed’s Atlas Coelestis (1729)
  • Messier objects, bright galaxies and the Milky Way
Open the Celestial Sphere →

Horizon Sky

The same sky seen from where we stand, at the center of the sphere on a flat disk of Earth. Set your latitude, the date and the time, and watch the sphere turn overhead.

  • Latitudes from pole to pole, with famous observing sites
  • The Sun, Moon and planets for any date, with the Moon's phases
  • Run time forward or back, or drag the Sun along the ecliptic
  • Step by sidereal days to watch planets trace retrograde loops
  • Click the sky to measure altitude and azimuth on a scale
Open the Horizon Sky →

Lunar Globe

A 3D Moon built from Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter images and laser altimetry, with elevation everywhere on the surface.

  • Thousands of named craters, maria and mountains
  • Apollo and other landing sites
  • Sharp close-ups of about 30 famous regions
Open the Lunar Globe →

Mars Globe

Mars in Viking color or THEMIS infrared, shaped by MOLA elevation data, with named features across the planet.

  • Landers and rovers from Viking to Perseverance
  • Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris and other major features
  • Close-ups of 26 regions, including Gale and Jezero craters
Open the Mars Globe →

Hubble Ultra Deep Field

The full-resolution Hubble Ultra Deep Field, with about 10,000 galaxies, some seen as they were less than a billion years after the Big Bang.

  • Pan and zoom to Hubble's full 0.03-arcsecond detail
  • Hover over any galaxy for its redshift and light-travel time
  • Mark galaxies and record their pixel and sky positions
  • Measure sizes in arcseconds and kiloparsecs
Open the Deep Field →
